## What happened

A password reset email you did not request usually means someone entered your email address on a login page. That does not always mean they are inside your account. It does mean your address is being tested.

The safest response is to slow down, avoid clicking anything in the email, and check the account directly from the official website or app.

## What this means for you

This could be harmless. It could also be the first step in an account takeover attempt. Attackers often test whether an account exists, then try old passwords, leaked passwords, or fake support messages.

You are most at risk if you reuse the same password on multiple sites or if the account does not have multi-factor authentication turned on.

## What to do right now

- Do not click the reset link unless you personally requested it.
- Open the company’s official app or website yourself.
- Change the password if the account is important.
- Turn on multi-factor authentication.
- Check recent login activity if the service offers it.
- Watch for follow-up texts, calls, or emails pretending to be support.

## How to stay safe

Use a unique password for every important account. A password manager makes this easier because you only need to remember one strong master password.

For your most important accounts, use a hardware security key if the service supports it. Security keys make many phishing attacks fail because the attacker cannot simply copy a code from you.

## Common mistake

People often click the email because it looks official. Instead, treat the email like an alarm. Go directly to the account through a trusted route and check from there.

## Final read

An unexpected reset email is a warning light, not proof of disaster. Respond carefully, secure the account, and assume follow-up scams may arrive next.
